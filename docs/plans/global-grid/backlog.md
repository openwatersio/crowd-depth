# Backlog

The work that takes the pipeline from a working point store to a planet rebuilt daily on cheap hardware, in priority order. Evidence for each item is in [rebuild-cost.md](rebuild-cost.md), [store-layout.md](store-layout.md), [qc-functions.md](qc-functions.md), [per-tile-pipeline.md](per-tile-pipeline.md), [channel-speckle.md](channel-speckle.md) and [surface-statistic.md](surface-statistic.md). The phases in the [plan](README.md#phases) still apply. This list orders the work inside them.

## 1. Fix what fails today

- [x] **Corroboration join runs out of memory.** `cells.CLASSIFY` joins each consensus shoal to every sounding in its 3″ block, which ran out of memory in the bay's `grid cells` at 3.7 GB and on the densest tile at 2 GB. Joining against one row per distinct depth in the block, with its least and greatest vessel, is exact while one reading of the same depth corroborates a shoal and takes 24 s on that tile. A mooring block later made even that a join of 96 billion pairs; see "Bound cells memory on the densest tiles".
- [x] **Ingest can't resume.** A multi-hour import is one transaction, so a single late R2 error discards it. Commit per batch, and record progress so a rerun skips committed files.
- [x] **Coverage is half of ingest.** Rule 4's coverage step took 9,724 s of 19,153 s for Chesapeake. Two parts are slow: a per-file overlap query (1.4 s per file on R2), and `covers()` scanning `points` twice for each nested pair (about 2 s each), though 49,258 of the 50,725 overlapping NOAA pairs nest in time and only 28 files have ever been covered. Find every candidate pair in one query, then check containment in groups of up to 5M points with one scan per group. This takes 136 s on the Chesapeake's 18,055 files.
- [x] **Output isn't deterministic.** `placed` in `cells.CLASSIFY` takes `arg_min(position, time)`, and ties at the same depth and time resolve by thread order, so unchanged inputs give different bytes. Break ties on a stable key. Per-tile change detection depends on this.

## 2. Calibrate vessels

Per-vessel vertical offsets are the largest error in the grid. In Thimble Shoal Channel vessels read a median 3.8 m shallower than BlueTopo, from 0.6 to 5.4 m by vessel, and the current surface draws the channel 9.3 m too shallow. Without calibration, no per-pixel statistic draws the channel right. [vessel-calibration.md](vessel-calibration.md) shows offsets hold to about 0.15 m across the bay, except that a quarter of vessels step over time, by a median 0.74 m, and that crossings recover a vessel's BlueTopo offset to 0.09 m (median).

- [x] **Spike crossover offsets.** Done in [vessel-calibration.md](vessel-calibration.md): BlueTopo calibrates 234 of 303 Chesapeake vessels, crossings anchored to the rest recover held-out offsets to 0.09 m (median) and 0.31 m (90th percentile), and offsets per step segment leave no pixel of the channel's safe surface more than 1 m too deep. The crossover work runs per 1° tile in under 5 s and 1.3 GB on the densest tile.
- [x] **Offsets table.** A `calibration` table of offset and standard error per vessel step segment, with segments found by step detection on per-transit offsets. Solve it jointly: BlueTopo and S-102 anchor segments with at least 50 flat-seabed pixels across 2 transits, weighted by their standard error, and every segment is solved at shared 2/3″ pixels by robust least squares, as in [Vessel calibration](README.md#vessel-calibration). Each tile adds its terms to a sparse system with one unknown per segment. A crossover offset counts as calibrated from 5 crossing transits. No scale factor until deeper water is in the set. Applied before gridding. A calibration change dirties every tile the segment crossed, so republish only offsets that move by more than their standard error.
- [x] **Bound calibration's memory.** `grid calibrate` reads the store in units of soundings (now 1.25M), banded by 6″ of latitude in dense tiles, picks each pixel's reference a tile at a time, and searches for stuck runs one vessel of one tile at a time. Its stuck runs come out the same on every run. DuckDB's Linux build allocates through a bundled jemalloc whose freed pages the limit doesn't count; the grid has it hand them back at once, which took up to 0.9 GB off calibrate's reduce. Other commands' memory figures haven't been measured with it. On the Chesapeake (303 vessels, 98.5M soundings) calibrate peaked at 1.70 GB at a 768 MB limit in a Linux container, against more than 4 GB at the 4 GB default before; after tide reduction, it ran out of memory at 768 MB and peaked at up to 2.4 GB at 1 GB, until "Bring calibrate under 2 GB" below. What's left past the limit is mostly the stuck-run search's, outside jemalloc: glibc's per-thread arenas and Python. Two glibc arenas (`MALLOC_ARENA_MAX=2`) took 0.16 GB more, at a fifth more time in that search. Leaving lost readings out of the medians added solves since; "Bring calibrate under 2 GB" measures them again.
- [ ] **Anchor to Seascape's surveyed sources.** Calibrate against any surveyed source in Seascape, not just BlueTopo and S-102. This waits on quality layers in Seascape: calibration needs each pixel's uncertainty, source and survey date to pick flat, trusted and current seabed, as BlueTopo's uncertainty band does here.
- [x] **Reduce tide before calibrating.** Even a nearest-gauge correction cuts the spread of per-transit offsets from 0.31 to 0.23 m, which tightens offsets and step detection.
- [ ] **Repeat calibration in a sparse area and on rocky bottom,** where crossings are few and anchors are only at the network's edges.
- [x] **Replace the 10th percentile with `median − 1.75σ`** over per-vessel medians, with σ combining each sounding's correction uncertainty with the spread of vessels around a local plane, published per pixel for `M_QUAL` and CATZOC, and drop the guard on the shoalest sounding. With tide and offsets corrected, it draws 0.3% of Thimble Shoal Channel's pixels more than 1 m deeper than the shoalest BlueTopo cell under them, with a channel bias of −1.2 m against −1.9 m for the current surface ([surface-statistic.md](surface-statistic.md)).
- [x] **Mark interpolated cells.** Surface pixels interpolated within 200 m of tracks are marked in `M_QUAL` and get a σ that widens with distance from the nearest track, as in [Interpolation](README.md#interpolation).
- [x] **Drop the guard on the shoalest sounding.** Carry hazards through confirmed shoals, which publish as soundings anyway. In the channel the guard alone draws a third of pixels under 9 m.
- [x] **Set aside transits that lost the bottom.** A stray transit reading more than 2 m shallower than its segment and under half of what its segment reads there has lost the bottom: its soundings set no depths and draw no surface, and publish as unconfirmed shoals. On the Chesapeake it marks 139 files from 11 vessels, among them a vessel reading 0.5 m in 8 m of water at the bay mouth. Over tiles 45463 and 45823, pixels drawn 0 to 2 m or drying where BlueTopo is 2 m or deeper fall from 39,019 to 18,508, and along those transits from 13,985 to 1,010; false shoals over 2 m fall from 8.1% to 5.8% of voted pixels, and pixels over 1 m too deep rise from 4.2% to 4.3%.
- [x] **Set aside runs and months where a sounder lost the bottom.** A transit whose median reads true can still hold a stuck run. Calibration sets aside a vessel's month in a 1° tile when a fifth of its judged pixels there read lost, so a sounder that lost the bottom in one place keeps its soundings in another, and a tile with under 100 judged pixels takes the month's verdict across its tiles: with stuck runs and stretches, 2.54M soundings on the Chesapeake store, where judging whole months set aside 3.05M and dropped one vessel's Gulf transit in 15 tiles where most of its pixels read the bottom. For vessels with any month at 5% or more it also finds runs of 30-reading windows, 27 of them lost and their middle 80% within 0.4 m, joined into runs of 60 windows across 200 m; the seabed is the survey's or, where shallower or there's none, three or more vessels' median. Their soundings set no depths and publish as unconfirmed shoals from 0.5 m.
- [x] **Keep lost readings out of calibration's medians.** Anchor rows took any reading within 12 m of the reference, and transit offsets every reading, so a vessel that often lost the bottom was calibrated too shallow and corrected too deep. Each solve now judges every transit-pixel median by the lost-bottom test, and the next solve leaves the lost ones out, for up to three more solves. Step finding leaves them out too, and a step into a stretch stays only if the stretch's readings follow the seabed. On the Chesapeake store ROSEP-607cc5a5 goes from −3.1 and −3.4 m to one segment at −2.3 m, what its readings that aren't lost read, and ROSEP-a247eccd from −7.1 to −1.8 m. Over the 15 Gulf tiles, which only ROSEP-607cc5a5 sounded, pixels more than 1 m too deep fall from 63.4% to 42.5% and bias from +0.55 to −0.19 m, while pixels under 1 m over 2 m or more of water rise from 0.51% to 1.29%. Over the 193 baseline tiles coverage stays at 66.4%, pixels more than 1 m too deep go from 6.99% to 7.01%, false shoals over 2 m from 4.21% to 4.29%, and held-out false shoals from 8.66% to 8.77%. A real move in a stretch that's mostly lost can still lose its step, and a vessel whose medians are mostly lost isn't helped.
- [x] **Catch lost-bottom readings in a tile that passes the month rule.** A searched vessel's lost readings over a flat, well-surveyed pixel are set aside however much they wander, each unbroken stretch of them that reaches 20 m at 0.5 m/s or faster. On the Chesapeake store it and the rule for thin tiles set aside 70,300 more soundings of nine vessels; one vessel's Gulf cells that publish 0 m over a median deeper than 2 m fall from 727 to 208, and false shoals over 2 m in its 15 Gulf tiles from 8.2% to 5.1% of covered pixels, 4.26% to 4.22% over the 193 Chesapeake benchmark tiles.
- [ ] **Better σ.** Per-vessel uncertainty from calibration residuals, plus spread around a local plane, so slopes don't widen σ as noise does. CUBE/CHRT does this formally and suits dense areas.
- [ ] **Leave uncalibrated vessels out of the median** where calibrated vessels sounded the pixel, and keep them, marked uncalibrated, where they're the only data. In Tangier Sound it took the median's false shoals from 19.6% to 9.6% ([surface-statistic.md](surface-statistic.md#sparse-water)).
- [ ] **A slope floor.** Use the shoalest accepted sounding as a floor only on pixels where σ_a, the window spread of vessel medians, is well above σ_b, their spread around a local plane: that is, on slopes. `median − 1.75σ` leaves 1.1% of Thimble Shoal Channel's slope pixels and 13% of Tangier Sound's more than 1 m too deep; measure the floor against both before adopting it ([surface-statistic.md](surface-statistic.md#findings)).
- [x] **Keep a lone track's coverage measured.** The band beside a lone track reaches two pixels past its soundings. Grown a pixel into the measured part, as water interpolated between tracks is, it covered the track's own strip, and the bay's measured coverage fell from 2,302 to 1,734 km². The band past a pixel from a sounding stays interpolated but isn't grown into the strip, which takes the bay's measured coverage to 2,301 km² ([surface.md](surface.md#lone-tracks)).
- [ ] **Smooth the datum separation between gauges.** MLLW above NAVD88 comes from the nearest CO-OPS gauge, so it jumps 0.42 m across Pocomoke Sound, from −0.37 m to Onancock's −0.79 m. Calibration anchors to BlueTopo with the same rule, so a vessel anchored on either side of the seam carries it. Interpolate between gauges, or use VDatum where it covers the water ([surface-statistic.md](surface-statistic.md#sparse-water)).

## 3. Make the daily rebuild fit

In order: each step makes the next one measurable.

- [x] **Store sorted by tile.** `points` and `qc_flags` are sorted by tile within each 10° partition (DuckLake's `SORTED BY`), in 256 MB files with row groups of about 123k rows. Each ingest batch is written sorted, a run's small files merge sorted when it publishes, and `grid compact` rewrites each partition once per store layout. On the Chesapeake, the store goes from 4.18 GB in 251 files to 2.81 GB in 11, `grid compact` takes 81 s on this laptop at the 4 GB default, and its 92M-row partition sorts in 96 s at 1 GB in a 2 GB container, and cells on the two densest tiles take 7% and 16% less time on local disk. QC of every batch takes 258 s, against 143 s, since a batch's rows are spread through sorted files; a daily run QCs only new batches, before they merge. Per-file rules (rule 4's cover, QC of an old batch, a takeover) now read every row group's `file_id` or `batch_id`; run them against a run's new files or per-file summaries, as [store-layout.md](store-layout.md#recommendation) recommends. Still to measure: reads from R2, where the gains in requests and bytes are larger.
- [x] **Drop the stored `point_id`.** A sounding is its `(file_id, row)`, packed into one 64-bit key that the point macros compute and `qc_flags` stores, with `tile_id`, so flag reads filter by tile. A copy taking a file's points over moves their flags to its file id.
- [ ] **Re-sort partitions as daily files accumulate.** A run's final publish merges its files with the partition's other files under 256 MB, its last compacted file among them, so each run can rewrite up to 256 MB of a partition, and a run that stops after a checkpoint publish leaves its files unmerged. Measure that cost on R2, and decide when to rewrite a partition whole with `grid compact` instead. QC of every batch after a QC version bump reads the whole store once per batch on a sorted store; QC by partition or by file instead before the planet's thousands of batches.
- [x] **Skip empty raster blocks in the surface.** The surface interpolates only the extent of the 512-pixel blocks with a sounding within the search radius, and blurs, guards, measures and writes only those blocks. The overlay cuts a tile's depth areas, contours and coverage on the window its depths and soundings reach, 16 pixels past them. On the Chesapeake's 212 tiles every surface raster is unchanged. The overlay's geometry is the same on 133 tiles; on the rest, contours and outlines traced from another origin start elsewhere and differ by slivers at most 0.39 px thick (16 tiles over 0.1 px), in both directions: no depth band's area moves by more than 1.3 px² of the bay's 24M. A tile under 10k soundings takes a median 2.8 s against 5.7 s, and one of 10k to 100k 4.8 s against 7.7 s; the densest tiles are dominated by their soundings, not their rasters.
- [ ] **Drop or defer the surface COG's overviews.** Overviews take about 1.2 s of a small tile's 2.8 s, computed across the whole tile at every level. Nothing in the pipeline reads them; Seascape, which will take these tiles as a source, may. Build them only for the tiles Seascape reads, or when it reads them.
- [x] **Hole filling in cells.** It took 3,412 s of the bay's 2 h 59 min build. Cells hold only water vessels sounded, and the surface does the interpolating.
- [x] **Read a tile's points once per cells run.** `active_points_in` reruns the dedupe for every band unit (6 × about 20 s on the densest tile), and the `SOURCES` write is a separate pass (122 s). Read once and derive both.
- [x] **Per-tile pipeline.** `grid tiles` rebuilds cells, surfaces and depth areas per 1° tile, web tiles per z8 tile, one low-zoom run, and byte-copies them into one PMTiles archive, as a Snakemake workflow ([per-tile-pipeline.md](per-tile-pipeline.md)). A tile reruns when its fingerprint moves ([manifest.py](../../../packages/grid/src/grid/manifest.py), from `lake.file_tiles`), its neighbors' and web tiles' jobs rerun after it, and a code change reruns everything. The archive is assembled only once every dirty job has finished, and a tile emptied by withdrawals has its outputs removed. Jobs fork from a warm worker, and declare memory and threads by their tile's soundings. On the Chesapeake it builds the same surfaces, cells and depth areas as `grid cells` and `grid overlay`, and replaying the last arrival day reruns exactly the 8 tiles it touched, their neighbors and their web tiles. With `--publish` it uploads the archive and each tile's surface, cells and depth areas that changed, named by content behind a manifest written last, so a daily run uploads only the tiles it rebuilt. Still open: the memory items below, and dirty work below 1°.
- [x] **Reduce only new files in calibration.** `grid calibrate` keeps each file's per-pixel medians in `lake.file_medians`, with a fingerprint of what they were made from in `lake.file_median_inputs`: the file's soundings per tile and their batches, its track flags, the tide cells its soundings fall in (`lake.file_tide_cells`, found once per file) and their stations' levels on the days it covers, and the code. A cell `grid tide` adds for a day's new files moves no other file: the day's 365 arrivals add 5 cells to one tile of 3,800 older files, and replaying the day with those cells taken out and put back still reduces the 365. The first run on a store also finds every file's tide cells, one more pass over the points (about 1 min on the Chesapeake under load). A run reduces the files whose fingerprint moved, and the files dedupe weighs against one that moved, came or went: the higher file_ids of its source and vessel over its time (rule 4), and for an Open Waters file the NOAA files of its vessel over its time (rule 5). Only files that haven't moved are paired with ones that have, so a run with nothing new costs time in proportion to the files, even for a vessel of 100,000 overlapping files (0.09 s). The solve runs on all the kept medians. On the Chesapeake on the Mac Studio (18,423 files, 20.9M medians), the reduce takes 41 to 56 s for every file, 15 to 26 s after a day's 365 arrivals (8 tiles, which hold 85% of the soundings) and 0.3 s with nothing changed, over two runs of each; whole runs take 161 to 213 s, 132 to 158 s and 124 to 142 s, the rest being the solve, the stuck-run search and publishing. A full run's offsets, outliers, file offsets and stuck runs are identical to before, and after hiding and restoring that day, the incremental run's are identical to a full recompute's, medians included. On Linux at a 1 GB limit (Ubuntu VM, 4 GB cgroup) a run peaks at 2.16 GB with nothing new, 2.18 GB after the day and 2.41 GB reducing every file again, against 2.21 GB for the code before, which reduced every file each run; neither fit a 2 GB worker then (see "Bring calibrate under 2 GB"). A day's `grid tide` also refetches gauge months that aren't final yet, which moves the files on any day whose levels change. A day still rereads its tiles' points whole for the vessels it touched; read only the new files' rows if dense tiles make that slow.
- [ ] **Dirty work below 1° in dense tiles.** Mark the bands that new files touch, plus one band each side, and rebuild surfaces in windows of about 1/8° with a 200 m margin, so daily cost follows each day's new tracks. Compare with an adaptive quadtree that splits tiles until each holds under N soundings.
- [ ] **Hierarchical low zooms.** z5–z7 per z5 tile, then z0–z4 from those, before the planet's feature volume makes one run slow. Test that shoals survive at z4–z7.
- [x] **Bound cells memory on the densest tiles.** The cells job of `grid tiles` for tile 45463 (28M soundings) now peaks at 1.74 GB anon in 190 s, against 1.93 GB in 604 s on main, and finishes in a 2 GB Linux cgroup at 1.74 GB in 202 s. Its cells and flags, and those of its eight neighbors and 46183, are byte-identical to main's, and its shoals the same rows in another order. What held the time was `consensus_rejects`: one mooring block of 45463 holds 327,667 shoal candidates and 291,085 distinct depths, and joining every candidate to every depth within the tolerance made 96 billion pairs, 334 of the job's 604 s, spilling everything else DuckDB held. Splitting by band or by block can't bound a single block, so the join now checks each candidate against only the nearest other vessel's depth below and above it, by ASOF join: within the tolerance only grows as a depth gets nearer, so that finds the same shoals, and the statement takes 4 s. What held the memory was DuckDB's limit: the job ran at it through every classify step, with about 0.7 GB outside it, and neither two glibc arenas, trimming after each statement nor two threads moved the peak by more than 0.08 GB. With the join cheap, a cells job's DuckDB gets 1 GB (`tiles.CELLS_MEMORY_LIMIT`) rather than 1.2 GB, which takes 0.2 GB off each dense tile's peak at the same speed; at 800 MB, 45463's per-pixel medians in `cells.sigmas`, which can't spill, run out. In 2 GB cgroups 45823 peaks at 1.53 GB, 46183 at 1.47 GB and 45464 at 1.37 GB. `tiles.MEMORY_MB` for cells is refit to 1.75 GB from 1M soundings and 2.25 GB from 10M, 0.36 GB over these peaks, the gap between OrbStack's and Colima's figures for main. Measured on OrbStack's Linux VM (aarch64, 4 threads), where main already fit, at 1.93 GB, against 2.29 GB on Colima. Still open: the 2 GB cgroup on another VM than OrbStack's, a tile whose `sigmas` medians outgrow 1 GB, which fails where 1.2 GB might not, and x86 cores.
- [x] **Bring surface jobs, and all overlay jobs but one, under 2 GB.** Every surface job and all but one overlay job of `grid tiles` on the Chesapeake run in a 2 GB Linux cgroup, with outputs byte-identical to before. Each job caps GDAL's block cache at 64 MB, which otherwise takes 5% of the machine's memory: 0.6–0.8 GB of a sparse surface's 1 GB. A surface rasterizes its soundings and confirmed shoals a block at a time, not over the whole tile, closes its DuckDB connection before gridding, and gives DuckDB one thread, since the per-pixel medians can't spill and each thread adds memory past DuckDB's limit. An overlay reads its neighbors' cells and shoals as views over their files, not copies, decodes gdal_contour's bands one feature at a time, and frees its rasters before the bands are validated and each level's union once it's used. Measured per job, anon memory: surfaces from a median of 1,033 MB to 266 MB and a maximum of 2,358 MB to 1,852 MB (2.05 GB for 45463 run alone, which still finishes in a 2 GB cgroup), overlays from a median of 629 MB to 487 MB and a maximum of 3,221 MB to 2,187 MB. One thread costs the densest surface 100–200 s against two. `tiles.MEMORY_MB` is refit to these figures, and cells' to theirs on Linux.
- [ ] **Validate a dense tile's contour bands in less memory.** Overlay 45464 (4.3M soundings) still needs 2.2 GB, and is killed in a 2 GB cgroup: `shapely.make_valid` on two gdal_contour bands of 0.6M and 0.9M points takes 1.4 GB in GEOS. Validating each polygon on its own takes half that, but gives up to 0.06% more area per band and lets bands overlap where gdal_contour writes a hole outside its shell; the `structure` method is lighter still and drops up to 2% of a band. Validating clusters of polygons whose envelopes touch matched on 9 of 10 bands. Find an exact split, or decide what such holes should mean.
- [x] **Bring calibrate under 2 GB.** On the tide-reduced Chesapeake, `grid calibrate` reducing every file again now finishes in a 2 GB Linux cgroup at a 768 MB limit, peaking at 1.44 GB in 627 s, and at 1 GB at 1.71 GB, with the same medians, segments, offsets as stored, stray transits and lost runs as before. On main it was killed in that cgroup at 1 GB, after the first solve, and ran out of DuckDB memory in its reduce at 768 MB. Three things held memory past the limit. Each sounding was checked against every lost run of its file in `corrected_points_in`, up to 5,402 for one file: a vessel's 5.3M soundings in one tile took more than 768 MB to correct, and every reader of the macro paid it; the runs are now merged into disjoint stretches and joined by day, as fast and the same row for row. The per-pixel and per-transit medians can't spill, and the values they hold come from glibc's malloc, outside the limit, whose per-thread arenas kept 1.3 GB after the queries that freed them; calibrate now takes them a tile or a batch of files at a time and hands the freed memory back after each. And the stuck-run search read a vessel's soundings in a tile at once on every thread; it now reads them on 4, and runs its windows a batch of files at a time. The solve, Python's arrays and the reference pixels were never the problem. The offsets solved differ from main's by up to 5e-13 m, as main's differ from run to run, from sums whose order the threads set. Deploying it reduces every file and finds every stuck run again, then reruns every tile job, as the macro is part of their code; rolling back past it needs the old 3 GB worker. Those jobs read the new join too: on the same frozen catalog, cells for 45463 and its eight neighbors and 46183, and the surface for 45463, wrote byte-identical cells, flags and surface, and the same shoal rows in another order, and peaked within 0.12 GB of main (45463's cells at 2.29 GB against 2.30 GB, its surface at 2.00 GB against 2.05 GB), in about the same time. Still open: a vessel's soundings in one tile are read at once in the stuck-run search (5.3M here); the reduce and solve run on every thread, measured at 18; publishing compares each sounding of a file with a changed run against all of that file's changed runs, which is time rather than memory but unbounded when most runs change; and calibrate on x86 cores.
- [ ] **Bound QC memory per file, if memory proves an issue.** Track QC holds a batch's soundings and one file's working arrays in Python, outside DuckDB's limit, at about 200 bytes a sounding: 1.66 GB peak for NOAA's largest Chesapeake file (3.5M soundings) at a 500 MB limit, and about 16 GB for a file of 80M. If a worker runs short, stream a batch from DuckDB file by file and judge a large file a few transits at a time (each 7-day split is a clean cut), or cap soundings per file with an alert.
- [ ] **Measure the planet.** Tile count, density and daily spread are in [planet-size.md](planet-size.md), estimated from NOAA's track index. Still to measure: reads from DuckLake on R2 after compaction, timings on cheap x86 cores, the densest tiles' memory, and upload cost of the daily archive (the Chesapeake's went up from the Mac Studio at about 4.1 MB/s: [per-tile-pipeline.md](per-tile-pipeline.md#publishing)). Re-run the estimate in [rebuild-cost.md](rebuild-cost.md) against them.

## 4. Clean up QC and seams

These change results, so each bumps `QC_VERSION` or `GRID_VERSION` and triggers a full rebuild. Batch them where possible.

- [x] **QC as pure functions.** Track QC is numpy ([qc.py](../../../packages/grid/src/grid/qc.py)), with thresholds, cell resolution, interpolation radius and portrayal in [definitions.json](../../../packages/grid/src/grid/definitions.json) and test vectors in [qc_vectors.json](../../../packages/grid/tests/qc_vectors.json), which the plugin's TypeScript port ([qc.ts](../../../packages/signalk-plugin/src/qc.ts)) passes too. On the full Chesapeake store (24,413 files, 98.5M soundings, 2.31M track flags) its flags match the SQL's except for 84 soundings, all unconfirmed: three stuck runs that cross UTC midnight are flagged whole, and five shoal spikes sit beside one of them. QC of the whole store takes 53 s at a 2.0 GB peak, against 204 s at 2.35 GB for the SQL. New QC rules are written against these functions.
- [x] **Reject lost-bottom readings from the crowd's own evidence.** QC uses no reference surface. Along each track, a sounding deeper than twice the depth before it (counted as at least 10 m) plus 5 m, plus 0.25 m per metre between them, judged against the last sounding that wasn't and from both directions (from one, at a transit's start or end), is `steep`. In consensus, a sounding deeper than twice the deepest other vessel's vote nearby (counted as at least 10 m) plus 10 m is `consensus_far_deep`, with as few as one other vessel. On the Chesapeake, with the earlier rules, these reject 282,381 of the 284,504 soundings (99.3%) that a Seascape deep-side check had rejected (steep 168,776; consensus_deep 83,544; spike 17,324; consensus_far_deep 9,255; cell_deep 3,327; jump 155), and 15,922 of FARSND-024883f7's 15,979 in file 24377. What slips through is mostly ROSEP-22216fd2 (1,017 soundings, 59–267 m), ROSEP-8670beba (427, 82–105 m) and FARSND (173). In random samples of 20,000 rejects each, BlueTopo or another vessel's accepted sounding backs 4 steep rejects (0.02%) and 159 consensus_far_deep rejects (0.8%). The residual is a lone vessel's track that is smooth but wrong, deep all the way, where no other vessel sounded.
- [ ] **Give the one-other-vessel rule a distance term.** `consensus_far_deep` with one other vessel compares against that vessel's vote anywhere in the 3×3 blocks, about 270 m, with no allowance for distance, so a real channel beside a shelf another vessel sounded can be rejected on steep shores, where the steep check would allow the slope. Scale its limit with the distance to the nearest of that vessel's soundings, as the steep check does, and measure it on a steep coast such as Lynn Canal.
- [ ] **An unconfirmed fallback for steep rejects at transit edges.** A stretch at a transit's start or end is judged from one side only, so real deep water where a file starts or ends beside a steep rise is rejected with no way back. Keep those as a deep-side `review` or `unconfirmed` flag that another vessel's sounding there can clear.
- [ ] **A plausibility cap from the crowd's own data.** 2,123 soundings a Seascape deep-side check rejected still pass, mostly ROSEP-22216fd2 (1,017 at 59–267 m) and ROSEP-8670beba (427 at 82–105 m): smooth, deep all the way, with no other vessel close enough to judge them. Reject a sounding far deeper than any other vessel recorded within a few kilometres.
- [x] **Readings under 0.5 m don't corroborate.** They neither confirm nor are confirmed in consensus or cell confirmation, and keep their `review` flag. On the Chesapeake, 2,077 such readings lose a `cell_confirmed` flag, and 226,719 more stay unconfirmed shoals. Still open: 16,245 cells publish a depth under 0.5 m in water Seascape charts deeper than 5 m, most of them one vessel's, where those readings are the cell's ordinary soundings rather than candidates.
- [ ] **Widen the cell tolerance for candidate shoals** to max(2 m + 5%, 3 × MAD). Removes 77–89% of the holes that speckle busy channels, and nothing is dropped in sparse areas. Re-measure after calibration, since vessel offsets cause many of these candidates.
- [ ] **Keep stuck runs for review.** The flatline check rejects a run of one depth held over 1 km of track, which also rejects a moored boat's honest constant depth as its GPS wanders. Mark those runs "review" instead of "reject", so they stay out of depths and areas as now, but calibration or a stationary-boat rule can reinstate them later.
- [ ] **Catch sounders stuck reading shallow.** In Tangier Sound one calibrated vessel logged 19,000 soundings in 2003 at a raw 2.5–2.8 m in 9–22 m of water. The flatline check needs a constant reading and this one wandered by 0.3 m, the deep-side rules look the other way, and consensus needs three other vessels, which sparse water rarely has. With it and two uncalibrated vessels in, every statistic drew over a fifth of the area as false shoals. Try a file-level check for a near-constant reading far shoaler than the reference or the other vessels, and measure its false alarms on real shoals ([surface-statistic.md](surface-statistic.md#sparse-water)).
- [x] **Score track noise.** Keep the soundings of a track with a large local spread for consensus, but don't let them set depths, cap pixels or punch holes, so one-sided spike rejection doesn't throw out the true bottom. A track whose soundings zig-zag by more than 10% of its depth is `noisy`: its deep side votes in consensus but sets no depths, neither votes in nor caps the surface, corroborates no shoal and doesn't calibrate the vessel, and its shoal side stays unconfirmed shoals that neither confirm nor are confirmed. On the Chesapeake it marks 109,277 soundings noisy and keeps 45,623 as such shoals.
- [ ] **Seams between tiles.** Grid each tile with a few pixels of margin and crop after the blur and shoal guard, let consensus votes read across tile edges, then re-measure. This makes cells depend on their neighbors, so design it with the dirty-work item above.
- [ ] **Record GNSS quality.** The plugin sends fix method, HDOP, satellite count and horizontal accuracy per sounding where Signal K has them, the API accepts them, and ingest stores them as nullable `points` columns. QC then flags soundings without a fix, rejects or down-weights poor positions, and uses horizontal accuracy as each sounding's horizontal uncertainty, as in [Quality control](README.md#2-quality-control). NOAA's CSVs carry none of this, so it only ever covers Open Waters submissions. Two additions: ellipsoidal height per sounding, where the receiver gives it, as the antenna's altitude plus the geoidal separation (`navigation.gnss.antennaAltitude` and `navigation.gnss.geoidalSeparation`), optional, so the grid can later reduce depths to the ellipsoid without a tide model; and, where the receiver gives no horizontal accuracy, an estimate from the fix class and DOP (an RTK fix to a few centimetres, a differential fix to about 1 m, an autonomous fix to HDOP times about 5 m), marked as estimated.
- [ ] **Mask structures.** The Chesapeake Bay Bridge's piers draw as a line of 0.6–3 m shoals, from real echoes off the piers, and around Norfolk's piers and berths, echoes off piers and moored hulls that many vessels read are confirmed as shoals and cap the surface at pier depth: on that tile, 4,117 pixels with ten or more vessels draw a median 15 m shoaler than the survey. Mask soundings on bridges, piers, berths and pylons from OSM or ENC structure layers, and the water beside them, where vessels read echoes off moored hulls, so QC rejects them and they neither confirm nor cap the surface. The median check already keeps echoes far shoaler than the other vessels from capping; those within its tolerance still do.
- [x] **Cap the surface only at confirmed shoals near the median.** A confirmed shoal stays confirmed only within 2 m + 5% of the other vessels' median in its pixel; in Norfolk's harbour that takes the drying surface from 10.1% to 0.1%. Of the shoals it outvotes, 2 to 6% sit where the survey also reads shallow; measure isolated rocks in busy water further ([surface-statistic.md](surface-statistic.md#legibility)).
